THE CENTRAL MOVEMENT

See the relations, not just the elements.

When we read, we do not merely recognize words. We connect them into relations and construct a model of what is happening.

When we calculate in chess, we do not merely name moves. We track pieces, reach, constraints, forces, changes, and the new possibilities created by each transformation.

SEE → RELATE → MODEL → PROJECT → UPDATE

The domains are different. The relational architecture is similar.

PART I

Forces of Reading

Reading begins with visual information, but comprehension goes beyond decoding. Words activate knowledge, enter relationships, and become part of a developing model.

01

Decode

Visual marks become recognizable linguistic units.

02

Activate

Words activate knowledge, associations, expectations, and possibilities.

03

Relate

Words become meaningful through relationships with other elements.

04

Model

Relations accumulate into a representation of the situation.

05

Infer

Open relations invite the reader to determine what is not directly given.

06

Predict

The current model projects possible future information and states.

07

Evaluate

New information is compared against the current model.

08

Update

The model is confirmed, modified, rejected, or left uncertain.

THE READING LOOP

INPUT → RELATE → MODEL → OPEN RELATION → INFER / PREDICT / SIMULATE → NEW INFORMATION → EVALUATE → UPDATE

The model is never necessarily finished. New information can confirm, add, disambiguate, modify, contradict, or leave uncertainty in the existing model.

THE TWO WHYS

Why can point in two directions.

WHY = BECAUSE OF WHAT?

Look backward toward a cause or source.

STATE ← CAUSE

WHY = FOR WHAT?

Look forward toward purpose, consequence, or intended state.

STATE → PURPOSE / CONSEQUENCE
PAST ← PRESENT → FUTURE

PART II

The Forces of Chess

Chess can be viewed as a changing relational system. The important question is not simply where a piece can move, but what it can reach and what its relations allow it to do.

REACH → OCCUPANCY → CONSTRAINT → FORCE → TENSE → RESULTANT → CHANGE → REACH

REACH

What can reach what?

Movement patterns describe what a piece could do geometrically. Actual reach depends on the position.

MOVEMENT PATTERN → POSITION → ACTUAL REACH

Occupancy and obstruction determine where reach stops. A change in the board therefore changes the reach map.

FORCE

What can the relation do?

Force is the capacity of a relation to change or constrain the position. It is not material value and it is not guaranteed success.

FORCE

What the relation can do.

RESULTANT

What the interaction actually produces.

Force is relational potential.

THE SIX FORCES

A vocabulary for relational transformation.

Push

Drives the position toward a response.

Pull

Draws toward a destination or state.

Redirect

Changes the direction or duty of an existing force.

Release

Removes a constraint and activates a capability.

Tension

Concentrates competing demands in a configuration.

Block

Prevents a capability from expressing itself.

These six forces are a vocabulary proposed by this framework, not an established chess taxonomy.

CHANGE

The real object of calculation.

Do not stop at:

“The bishop moved.”

Ask:

“What became possible?”

A move matters because it transforms the state of the position.

STATE₀ → CHANGE → STATE₁

The difference between the two states is what calculation needs to understand.

CALCULATION

Calculation is relational change across states.

MY CHANGE → OPPONENT'S AVAILABLE CHANGES → NEW STATE

Three questions underneath calculation.

Direction

Obstacle

What this framework is — and is not

This is a functional model, not a claim about a literal step-by-step neural architecture. It does not claim that reading and chess are neurologically identical, that the six forces form an established chess taxonomy, or that the framework replaces existing linguistic, cognitive, or chess theories.

Its value lies in whether it helps organize perception, explanation, and calculation.
