WORKING WITH THE GRAIN

Work with the mind.
Not against it.

An exploration of how thought may become easier when we stop fighting the structures it naturally uses and instead learn to work with them.

Read the book →

THE QUESTION

What if thinking becomes easier when we work with its structure?

Much of the effort of thinking can come from trying to force ourselves into a method that does not match the way our minds are already operating.

Instead of asking only how to control thought, we can ask another question:

What is the mind already doing here?

Once that structure becomes visible, it may become possible to cooperate with it rather than constantly resist it.

THE GRAIN

Every system has a direction.

The metaphor of a grain comes from the idea that some structures already have a natural direction. Wood has a grain. Water follows channels. A physical system has constraints that shape what can happen.

Thought also has structure: attention, association, memory, prediction, imagery, recognition, comparison, expectation, and correction.

UNDERSTAND THE STRUCTURE → WORK WITH THE STRUCTURE

THOUGHT IS NOT EMPTY SPACE

The mind is already doing something.

When we try to understand something, we do not begin from nothing. Previous knowledge, associations, expectations, memories, categories, and mental representations are already present.

A new piece of information interacts with what is already there.

NEW INFORMATION + EXISTING STRUCTURE → NEW MODEL

This means that learning and understanding can be approached as transformations of an existing model rather than the simple accumulation of isolated facts.

REPRESENTATION

Think with the representation that helps you.

Thought does not have to take one universal form. Depending on the problem, a person may use visual, spatial, verbal, conceptual, symbolic, motor, causal, temporal, or multimodal representations.

Visual

Constructing or manipulating an internal scene, shape, object, or arrangement.

Relational

Tracking what is connected to what, what depends on what, and what changes when a relation changes.

Conceptual

Organizing ideas, categories, principles, explanations, and abstractions.

The useful question is not necessarily “Which representation should everyone use?” but “Which representation allows this problem to become clearer?”

ATTENTION

Attention can follow structure.

Instead of trying to attend equally to everything, attention can be guided toward what changes the model.

WHAT CHANGED? → WHAT DOES IT AFFECT? → WHAT FOLLOWS?

A small detail may become important if it changes a central relationship. A large amount of information may remain irrelevant if it does not affect the model being constructed.

RECOGNITION

Recognition is structure becoming available.

Recognition does not necessarily mean recalling a complete answer. It can mean detecting a familiar structure, relation, pattern, or possibility.

What do I know here?

What object, pattern, structure, or situation does this resemble?

Who do I know here?

What agent, intention, role, or social relationship is represented?

QUESTIONS AS TOOLS

A good question can align attention with the structure.

Change

What changed?

Cause

What caused this?

Future

What will happen next?

Obstacle

What is preventing the desired state?

Mechanism

What could change the situation?

Purpose

What is this for?

THE FUTURE

Prediction may be a natural extension of understanding.

Once a model contains relationships, it can be projected forward. The question becomes what the current structure makes possible, likely, necessary, or constrained.

CURRENT MODEL → PROJECT → POSSIBLE FUTURE

Prediction does not mean certainty. It means using the current model to generate expectations that can later be confirmed, modified, rejected, or left unresolved.

BACKWARD AND FORWARD

Thought can move in both directions.

Backward

Trace from the present toward what explains it.

STATE ← CAUSE

Forward

Project from the present toward what may follow.

STATE → CONSEQUENCE

Cause inference and prediction can therefore be understood as two directions of the same broader operation: following a relation toward an unseen target.

SIMULATION

Change the model and see what follows.

Simulation goes beyond passive prediction. It deliberately changes a condition inside the model and traces the consequences.

IF X WERE DIFFERENT → WHAT WOULD CHANGE?

This allows counterfactual thinking, planning, problem solving, and exploration of possibilities.

UPDATE

A model must remain revisable.

Working with the grain does not mean trusting every first impression. A useful model must change when reality gives us a reason to change it.

PREDICTION → NEW INFORMATION → COMPARISON → UPDATE

The goal is therefore not to protect the model from contradiction. It is to make the model responsive to information.

THE PRACTICAL IDEA

Stop asking only “How should I think?”

Ask:

WHAT AM I ALREADY REPRESENTING?
↓
WHAT RELATIONS ARE PRESENT?
↓
WHAT IS UNCERTAIN?
↓
WHAT AM I EXPECTING?
↓
WHAT WOULD CHANGE THE MODEL?
↓
WHAT SHOULD I ATTEND TO NEXT?

THE CONNECTION

Work with the structure that is already there.

The broader framework developed in Forces of Reading and Chess follows a similar principle: understanding becomes possible by representing relations, projecting change, and updating the model.

SEE → RELATE → MODEL → PROJECT → UPDATE

The aim is not to force thought into a rigid procedure. It is to make the underlying structure visible enough to use.

About the book

Working With the Grain is an exploratory framework rather than a claim that cognition follows one fixed procedure. Its purpose is to provide useful ways of noticing and working with structures already present in thought.
