CONCEPTS

A vocabulary for
relations and change.

The core concepts used throughout Forces of Reading and Chess: models, open relations, reach, force, constraint, prediction, calculation, and change.

THE CENTRAL PRINCIPLE

Understanding begins with relations.

An isolated element tells us relatively little. Its role becomes clearer when we understand what it is related to, what those relations allow or prevent, and what happens when the state changes.

SEE → RELATE → MODEL → PROJECT → UPDATE

READING

Core concepts of reading.

Decode

Transform visual marks into recognizable linguistic units and words. Decoding is the entrance into reading, not the whole of comprehension.

Activate

A word activates knowledge, associations, memories, concepts, expectations, categories, and possible meanings.

Relate

Connect elements through relations such as cause, dependency, support, restriction, sequence, implication, or change.

Model

A structured internal representation of a situation, containing relevant objects, events, properties, relations, time, goals, causes, and uncertainty.

Open Relation

A relation whose value is not yet determined. It creates something for the reader to infer, predict, test, or leave unresolved.

Inference

Movement from available information toward something not directly given.

Prediction

Inference directed toward a future or not-yet-observed target.

Simulation

Altering a model condition and tracing what consequences would follow.

Update

Revising the current model when new information confirms, modifies, contradicts, or leaves an interpretation uncertain.

THE MODEL

Representation is structured.

A model does not have to be a mental picture. It may be spatial, visual, auditory, motor, conceptual, symbolic, propositional, causal, temporal, dynamic, multimodal, or a combination.

WORD → RELATION → SCENE → EVENT → CAUSE → GOAL → FUTURE

These are dimensions of representation rather than mandatory stages. A reader may represent a scene, a sequence of states, a causal dependency, an intention, or several of these together.

THE TWO WHYS

Why can point in two directions.

Backward

What caused this?

STATE ← CAUSE

The question traces a relation toward an earlier condition, source, or explanation.

Forward

What is this for?

STATE → PURPOSE / CONSEQUENCE

The question projects toward purpose, consequence, function, or a possible later state.

PAST ← PRESENT → FUTURE

CHESS

Core concepts of chess calculation.

Reach

What a piece can actually interact with or reach in the current position.

Occupancy

What is actually present on the relevant squares and how that presence changes available relations.

Constraint

Anything that limits a capability from becoming available action.

Attack

Hostile reachability: a relation through which one side can potentially act against another.

Support

Friendly reachability: a relation that can assist, protect, or reinforce another piece or square.

Force

The capacity of a relation to change or constrain the position.

Tense

The distinction between a force active in the current state and one that would become active after a possible change.

Resultant

What the interaction of relations actually produces.

Change

The difference between states: what became possible, impossible, opened, closed, removed, created, or redirected.

REACHABILITY

Many chess concepts can be understood through reach.

ATTACK = HOSTILE REACHABILITY
SUPPORT = FRIENDLY REACHABILITY
MOBILITY = AVAILABLE MOVEMENT REACHABILITY
RESTRICTION = REDUCED REACHABILITY
BLOCKADE = SUPPRESSED REACHABILITY
DISCOVERED ATTACK = HIDDEN REACHABILITY BECOMES AVAILABLE

THE SIX FORCES

Six proposed lenses for describing force.

Push

Drives the position toward a response or new state.

Pull

Draws toward a destination or state. A functional metaphor, not literal attraction.

Redirect

Changes the direction or duty of an existing force.

Release

Removes a constraint and activates a capability.

Tension

Concentrates competing demands within a position.

Block

Prevents a force or capability from expressing itself.

These six forces are a proposed vocabulary for analysis, not an established taxonomy of chess.

FORCE ≠ RESULT

Potential is not outcome.

Force

What a relation can do.

FORCE → POSSIBLE CHANGE

Resultant

What interacting relations actually produce.

INTERACTION → ACTUAL RESULT

An attack is not automatically a capture. A threat is not automatically an event. A force can be blocked, resisted, redirected, exchanged, or made irrelevant.

POSITION

A position is a state.

The position can be represented as a current state containing piece locations and the relevant relations between them.

S(now) → MOVE → S(next)

The next state becomes the new current state.

CALCULATION

Calculation is relational change across states.

STATE₀ → CHANGE → STATE₁

The important question is not merely: “What move can I play?”

What changes if I move this?

Then:

What does the new position allow?

THE CALCULATION LOOP

The vocabulary becomes a cycle.

REACH → OCCUPANCY → CONSTRAINT
→ FORCE → TENSE → RESULTANT
→ CHANGE → REACH

The loop is recursive because every meaningful change creates a new state with a new reach map.

DIRECTION · OBSTACLE · MECHANISM

Three questions organize purpose.

Direction

Where should the position go?

Obstacle

What prevents the desired state?

Mechanism

What transformation could overcome the obstacle?

THE BROADER ARCHITECTURE

Two domains, one general pattern.

Reading

WORDS → RELATIONS → MODEL

The model supports inference, prediction, simulation, evaluation, and updating.

Chess

PIECES → RELATIONS → POSITION

The position supports force analysis, calculation, prediction, and reconstruction after change.

AVAILABLE INFORMATION → MODEL → OPEN POSSIBILITY
→ PROJECTION → NEW INFORMATION → UPDATED MODEL

THE SHARED PRINCIPLE

Follow the relation through change.

In reading, an unresolved relation may lead to an inference or prediction. In chess, a changed state creates a new set of reachable relations.

SEE → RELATE → MODEL → PROJECT → UPDATE

The domains are not claimed to be neurologically identical. The framework proposes a functional analogy: both can be understood by tracking relations, possibilities, changes, and model updates.

THE CORE QUESTIONS

What is related?
What can change?
What follows?

See the relation. See what it can do. See what changes.

About this vocabulary

These concepts form a functional framework developed by Abdessamad Regragui. They are intended as tools for organizing attention, explanation, reading, and chess calculation. They are not presented as a literal neural architecture, a closed taxonomy of cognition, or an established taxonomy of chess.
