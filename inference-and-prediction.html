INFERENCE & PREDICTION

What is hidden?
What comes next?

Understanding does not stop at what is directly given. A model lets us infer what is missing, predict what may happen, simulate alternatives, and revise our interpretation when new information arrives.

THE BASIC MOVEMENT

From what is given to what is not yet determined.

A reader rarely receives every relation explicitly. The text provides some information while leaving other things unstated, uncertain, ambiguous, or still in the future.

AVAILABLE INFORMATION → UNSEEN TARGET

The unseen target might be a hidden cause, an omitted event, an unstated consequence, a character's intention, a future event, or simply the identity of something that has not yet been determined.

INFERENCE

Inference fills the open relation.

Consider:

John picked up the glass. He drank.

The text does not explicitly state every connection between the two sentences. The reader constructs the relation that makes the sequence coherent.

OPEN → INFER → RESOLVE → UPDATE MODEL

Inference is therefore not something reserved for difficult passages. It is part of ordinary comprehension.

OPEN RELATIONS

Not everything has to be resolved immediately.

A useful model can contain both what is known and what is unknown.

Known

There was a noise behind the door. Its location is represented, but its source is not.

Unknown

The source may have several possible explanations. The reader can maintain these candidates without prematurely choosing one.

KNOWN + UNKNOWN + CANDIDATES

New information can later resolve the relation:

NEW INFORMATION → RESOLUTION → UPDATED MODEL

AMBIGUITY

Sometimes several models remain possible.

Consider the sentence:

The man saw the woman with binoculars.

The phrase can support more than one interpretation. The man may have used the binoculars, or the binoculars may be associated with the woman.

AMBIGUITY → MULTIPLE CANDIDATE INTERPRETATIONS

Later information can resolve the ambiguity. But sometimes no resolution arrives. A good model can preserve uncertainty instead of inventing certainty.

PREDICTION

Prediction is inference aimed toward the future.

When a sentence begins:

The man reached for the...

the developing model generates expectations. An object, an action, a location, or a purpose may become relevant.

CURRENT MODEL → PROJECTED TARGET

Prediction is not certainty. It is an expectation that can be confirmed, modified, or rejected by what comes next.

THE READING LOOP

Prediction and comprehension continuously interact.

READ
↓
BUILD MODEL
↓
EXPECT
↓
RECEIVE NEW INFORMATION
↓
COMPARE
↓
UPDATE

New information can confirm what was expected, add something new, disambiguate an open relation, modify the model, contradict it, or leave the uncertainty unresolved.

THE TWO DIRECTIONS OF WHY

Why can point backward or forward.

Backward

What caused this state?

STATE ← CAUSE

The question searches for an earlier condition, event, or dependency that explains what is present.

Forward

What is this for?

STATE → PURPOSE / CONSEQUENCE

The question searches toward a goal, function, consequence, or future state.

PAST ← PRESENT → FUTURE

The same present information can therefore be used to search backward for a cause or forward toward a purpose or consequence.

SIMULATION

Prediction asks what may happen. Simulation changes the model.

Simulation goes beyond passive expectation. The model is altered and its consequences are traced.

MODEL → ALTER CONDITION → SIMULATE → CONSEQUENCES

A counterfactual question makes the operation explicit:

If X were different, what would change?

This becomes especially important in chess, where calculation consists of projecting possible transformations through future states.

MODEL REVISION

Later information can rewrite earlier understanding.

Reading is not a straight accumulation of facts. A later sentence can force the reader to revisit an earlier interpretation.

READ → BUILD → PROBLEM → REVISIT → REINTERPRET → CONTINUE

A contradiction may require the model to be revised. A pronoun may force a return to an earlier reference. A plot twist may reorganize the meaning of information that was already known.

The text is linear. The model does not have to be.

THE GENERAL ARCHITECTURE

Inference and prediction are movements through a model.

AVAILABLE INFORMATION
↓
MODEL
↓
OPEN POSSIBILITY
↓
PROJECTION
↓
NEW INFORMATION
↓
UPDATED MODEL

The important operation is not guessing randomly. It is following represented relations toward something that is not yet directly available.

READING AND CHESS

The same general architecture appears in calculation.

Reading

WORDS → RELATIONS → MODEL

The reader builds a model and uses it to infer hidden information and predict what follows.

Chess

PIECES → RELATIONS → POSITION

The player models the position and projects what can change through possible future states.

SEE → RELATE → MODEL → PROJECT → UPDATE

THE CORE QUESTION

Follow the relation through change.

What is given?
What is connected?
What is still open?
What follows?

About the framework

This is a functional framework for organizing ideas about reading, inference, prediction, and calculation. It is not presented as a literal description of neural architecture, nor as a replacement for established theories in cognitive science, linguistics, or chess.
