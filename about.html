ABOUT THE AUTHOR

Why I write about these things.

I am interested in what happens underneath activities that seem very different on the surface.

Reading looks like recognizing words. Chess looks like moving pieces. But underneath both, there are relationships, changing states, possibilities, expectations, and consequences.

My writing tries to make some of those structures explicit.

FORCES OF READING AND CHESS

From reading to calculation.

Forces of Reading and Chess is an exploration of a common functional architecture across two domains.

In reading, words become relations, relations become a model, and the model supports inference and prediction.

In chess, pieces form relations within a position. A move transforms that position, creating a new network of possibilities that must be read again.

WORKING WITH THE GRAIN

Understanding the structures already present in thought.

Working With the Grain explores the possibility that thinking can become more effective when we work with the structures our minds already use rather than constantly fighting them.

Representation, attention, recognition, prediction, simulation, and updating are treated as parts of a broader way of approaching thought.

A SMALL VOCABULARY

Relations before labels.

A recurring principle in my work is that labels become more useful when we understand the relationships underneath them.

What is related?

Identify the elements and the relationships connecting them.

What can change?

Identify the transformations that could alter the current state.

What follows?

Project the consequences of those transformations.

THE FRAMEWORKS ARE OPEN

These are models, not final theories.

The concepts presented on this website are functional frameworks. They are intended to organize attention and provide useful ways of describing thought and chess calculation.

They are not presented as literal neural architecture, established cognitive taxonomies, or replacements for existing scientific or chess theories.

The categories are therefore open to refinement, correction, comparison, and disagreement.
