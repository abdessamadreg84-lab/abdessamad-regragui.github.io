THE TWO WHYS

Why did it happen?
Why is it for?

The question “why?” can move in two different directions: backward toward a cause, or forward toward a purpose, consequence, or future state.

ONE WORD, TWO DIRECTIONS

“Why?” is not always asking the same thing.

When something happens, we can ask what produced it. But we can also ask what it is meant to produce, what it is for, or what follows from it.

PAST ← PRESENT → FUTURE

The present state becomes the point from which the mind searches in either direction.

WHY BACKWARD

What caused this?

The first form of “why?” searches for an earlier condition, event, action, or dependency that explains the current state.

STATE ← CAUSE
Why is the window broken?

The question may lead backward:

BROKEN WINDOW ← IMPACT ← OBJECT / EVENT

The mind traces the relation toward its source.

WHY FORWARD

What is this for?

The second form of “why?” searches toward purpose, function, consequence, or a possible future state.

STATE → PURPOSE / CONSEQUENCE
Why did he open the door?

The answer might point forward:

OPEN DOOR → ENTER ROOM → REACH SOMETHING

Here the question is not primarily asking what caused the opening. It is asking what the action is directed toward.

THE DIFFERENCE

Cause and purpose are not the same relation.

Cause

What produced the state or event?

BEFORE → NOW

The search moves toward an earlier condition that explains what is now present.

Purpose

What is the state or action directed toward?

NOW → AFTER

The search moves toward a goal, function, consequence, or intended future.

THE SAME PRESENT STATE

One state can support both questions.

Imagine a person running.

RUNNING

Backward

Why is he running?

RUNNING ← WHAT CAUSED IT?

Forward

Why is he running?

RUNNING → WHAT IS HE TRYING TO ACHIEVE?

The wording can be identical while the direction of inquiry is completely different.

IN READING

Readers constantly move backward and forward.

When reading a story, the reader may encounter an unexplained event and search backward for its cause.

EVENT → WHAT CAUSED THIS?

At the same time, the reader may see an action and project forward toward its purpose or consequence.

ACTION → WHAT IS THIS FOR?

Understanding therefore involves movement through the model in different directions, depending on what remains unresolved.

INFERENCE

Both questions search for an unseen relation.

In the backward case, the missing relation may be a cause. In the forward case, it may be a purpose or consequence.

AVAILABLE INFORMATION → UNSEEN RELATION

Backward

Search for what produced the present state.

Present

Represent what is currently known and unresolved.

Forward

Search for what the state leads toward.

CAUSE, DEPENDENCY, CONSEQUENCE

“Why?” is one example of a broader relational operation.

A relation does not have to be strictly causal. One state may depend on another, require it, enable it, constrain it, prevent it, support it, precede it, or change it.

Backward relations

Cause
Source
Origin
Precondition
Earlier dependency

Forward relations

Purpose
Function
Consequence
Possibility
Future dependency

These relations are not identical. They are different ways of connecting states and events across a model.

THE BRIDGE TO CHESS

Chess calculation also asks where a relation leads.

In reading, we may ask what caused the current state or what the current action is directed toward.

In chess, we can similarly trace relations backward or project them forward through a change of state.

Reading

PRESENT ← CAUSE

What explains this?

PRESENT → PURPOSE / CONSEQUENCE

What does this lead toward?

Chess

CURRENT POSITION ← PREVIOUS CHANGE

What produced this structure?

CURRENT POSITION → FUTURE STATE

What happens if this relation changes?

THE GENERAL PRINCIPLE

Follow the relation in the direction that matters.

SEE → RELATE → MODEL → PROJECT → UPDATE

The important question is not simply “why?” in isolation. It is:

WHAT IS THE CURRENT STATE?
↓
WHAT RELATION IS UNRESOLVED?
↓
DO I NEED TO TRACE BACK?
OR
DO I NEED TO PROJECT FORWARD?

THE TWO WHYS

What produced this?
What does this produce?

Backward to understand.
Forward to anticipate.

About the framework

This is a functional framework for organizing ideas about reading, inference, causality, prediction, and calculation. It is not presented as a literal description of neural architecture or as a replacement for established theories.
