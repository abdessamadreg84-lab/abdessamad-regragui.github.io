PART I · FORCES OF READING

Reading is more than recognizing words.

Reading can be understood as the construction, testing, and updating of a relational model.

WORDS → RELATIONS → MODEL → INFERENCE → PREDICTION → UPDATE

01 · DECODE

From visual marks to words.

Reading begins with visual information being transformed into recognizable linguistic units.

VISUAL INPUT → WORD → MEANING

But decoding is only the entrance to comprehension. Recognizing a word is not the same thing as understanding the situation in which it appears.

02 · ACTIVATE

A word opens a field of possibilities.

A word can activate knowledge, associations, memories, concepts, expectations, categories, sensory knowledge, social knowledge, and possible actions.

WORD + KNOWLEDGE + CONTEXT → AVAILABLE INTERPRETATIONS

Context selects. The sentence constrains. Prior knowledge supplies possibilities.

03 · RELATE

Information becomes useful through relationships.

Words do not remain isolated. They become meaningful through their relationships with other elements.

MAN → OPENED → DOOR

Relationships can include:

Produces

A produces B.

Depends

A depends on B.

Limits

A limits B.

Enables

A enables B.

Prevents

A prevents B.

Changes

A changes B.

These are examples of relationship types, not a closed taxonomy.

04 · MODEL

Relations accumulate into a model.

The reader constructs a representation of the situation. Depending on the text and the reader, this model may contain objects, people, places, events, properties, relations, time, causality, goals, and uncertainty.

A model may be spatial, visual, auditory, motor, conceptual, symbolic, propositional, causal, temporal, dynamic, or multimodal.

Scene

A represented state or configuration.

Sequence

Ordered states or events.

Process

States together with represented transformation.

Causal Process

Transformation together with represented causal dependency.

05 · THE OPEN RELATION

Comprehension contains unknowns.

Text often gives us part of a situation while leaving other relationships undetermined.

NOISE → BEHIND THE DOOR → SOURCE UNKNOWN

The correct model does not always need to invent an answer. It can contain what is known, what is unknown, and several possible candidates.

KNOWN + UNKNOWN + CANDIDATES

06 · INFERENCE

What is not stated can still be represented.

Inference is the movement from available information toward something that has not yet been directly determined.

The target may be a hidden cause, an omitted event, a spatial location, an unstated consequence, an intention, a future event, or another missing relationship.

OPEN → INFER → RESOLVE → UPDATE MODEL

Inference is therefore not restricted to difficult passages. It is part of ordinary comprehension.

07 · PREDICTION

The model projects what may come next.

Prediction can operate at many timescales: the next word, the next event, a character's action, or a possible causal consequence.

CURRENT MODEL → PROJECTED TARGET
READ → MODEL → EXPECT → RECEIVE → COMPARE → UPDATE

Prediction is an expectation, not a certainty. New information can confirm it, modify it, or reject it.

08 · EVALUATE

New information is compared with the model.

Reading is not simply the accumulation of information. New information can fit the existing model or create conflict.

NEW INFORMATION → COMPARE WITH MODEL → FIT / CONFLICT

When conflict appears, the reader may need to revise the interpretation.

CONFLICT → REVISE MODEL

09 · UPDATE

The model keeps moving.

Later information can change the interpretation of earlier information. A pronoun can force a return. A contradiction can require reconstruction. A twist can reinterpret what seemed established.

READ → BUILD → PROBLEM → REVISIT → REINTERPRET → CONTINUE

The text may be linear. The model does not have to be.

THE TIME AXIS

Information can point backward or forward.

PAST ← PRESENT → FUTURE

Past

What caused this? Where did it come from?

Present

What is actually happening? What hidden state explains it?

Future

What will happen? What consequence follows?

THE TWO WHYS

“Why?” can point in two directions.

Because of what?

Look backward toward the cause or source.

STATE ← CAUSE

For what?

Look forward toward purpose or consequence.

STATE → PURPOSE / CONSEQUENCE

Both are movements through relations toward an unseen target.

SIMULATION

What if the model were different?

Simulation changes a condition in the model and traces the consequences.

IF X WERE DIFFERENT → WHAT WOULD CHANGE?

This creates a bridge from comprehension toward counterfactual reasoning and, later, chess calculation.

REFERENCE & PEOPLE

The model can contain relationships between minds.

Reading also requires tracking reference: who or what does a word refer to?

SIGN → REFERENT

The reader can also model what another person knows, believes, wants, expects, or sees.

MODEL OF WORLD ≠ MODEL OF WHAT A CHARACTER BELIEVES

THE COMPLETE READING LOOP

Reading as a moving relational model.

INPUT + KNOWLEDGE + CONTEXT → REPRESENT → RELATE → MODEL → WHAT IS NOT DETERMINED? → INFER / PREDICT / SIMULATE → NEW INFORMATION → EVALUATE → CONFIRM / REVISE / REJECT / REMAIN UNCERTAIN → UPDATE MODEL → NEW OPEN RELATION → LOOP

THE CORE

See what is given.
See what is related.
See what is missing.
See what follows.

SEE → RELATE → MODEL → PROJECT → UPDATE

About the framework

This is a functional vocabulary for describing aspects of reading and comprehension. It is not intended as a literal neural architecture or as a replacement for established theories of reading and cognition.
